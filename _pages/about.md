I am a Biomedical Engineering PhD candidate with a background in physics, working on quantitative MRI and MR Fingerprinting in Prof. Nicole Seiberlich’s group at the [Michigan Institute for Imaging Technology and Translation (MIITT)](https://miitt.med.umich.edu/), University of Michigan. My work focuses on rapid and quantitative MRI, including MR Fingerprinting for abdominal imaging, multiparametric tissue characterization (T1, T2, T2*, T1ρ, PDFF), and reconstruction methods for multi-echo MRF data.

I am a core contributor to [OpenMRF](https://openmrf.org/), an open framework for MR Fingerprinting research and reproducible method development. Through this work, I have had the opportunity to collaborate with researchers across institutions and countries, as well as with academic and industry partners. I particularly enjoy the collaborative and practical side of research—bringing together different technical perspectives and working toward methods that can be used beyond a single research group.

I grew up in Germany and have also lived and studied in the United States, France, and Israel. I am fluent in German, English, and French.

Outside the lab, I spend most of my time biking, running, hiking, and camping, and enjoy brass music and sourdough baking.
